Research

My research asks how communities under uncertainty or strain collaborate, communicate, and increasingly rely on AI, and how those systems can be designed so that the people who depend on them can meaningfully shape and use them.

Community science & sustainability

Penn State · 2017–2023 (dissertation) · ongoing

The world's water resources are under strain, and around 70% of streams in the United States remain unassessed. Thousands of volunteer groups monitor their local waters, often without professional backgrounds in water science, which supplements government data and builds scientifically literate communities. My dissertation studied this practice as a socio-technical system. In a discovery study I conducted 15 semi-structured interviews, field observations with one group, and seven design hackathons, finding that citizen-based monitoring is a new form of community engagement and that these groups need a comprehensive technical infrastructure for data and collaboration. I then worked with The Commons on the design and evaluation of the Water Data Collaborative, a national online network for water-quality stakeholders, through five scenario-based participatory design sessions. My current work explores how AI can be integrated into this infrastructure to strengthen local knowledge rather than displace it.

Indigenous AI

With tribal communities · 2023–present

Large language models are changing how people learn about Indigenous histories and knowledge, but they can misrepresent data from communities that have long been marginalized, which raises serious questions of data sovereignty. Using a co-design approach, we worked directly with tribal communities across three iterations: think-aloud interviews with the Omaha Tribe on ChatGPT in tribal emergency management; a chatbot grounded in documents from the Sac and Fox tribe; and TribalLLM, which lets users report flood damage and draws on data from multiple tribes in a local database. Across the work we heard that disasters differ between tribes and need localized information, that chatbots must work for elders and people with disabilities, that tribal sovereignty must be represented accurately, and that trust is built through personal connections and introducing technology gradually.

Public health & wellbeing

2023–present

I design AI systems that make medically vetted information easier to reach for families and communities underserved by existing diagnostic infrastructure. With collaborators, I have studied how an LLM chatbot can support communication between caregivers and providers for neonatal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE), a rare and high-stakes condition, and how retrieval-augmented generation can balance health information-seeking. A newer, collaborative line of work on autism, still in its early stages, examines how generative and multimodal AI could support autistic and neurodiverse people and families through citizen-led behavioral review. Separately, I have also studied how patients and providers experience an AI-powered telehealth platform.

Community life & civic technology

2017–2023

Before I turned to AI, my work looked at how people and communities build and maintain relationships with the help of technology. I studied how older adults construct and maintain their social circles, how a local community conflict plays out around technology and place, how playful meaning-making can be prosocial, and how misinformation circulates within local communities. In a smaller study, I analyzed posts on the r/Blind subreddit to understand how people with visual impairment collectively construct the meaning of “independence,” and what that implies for assistive technology.

AI in interdisciplinary teams

IBM Research · Summer 2021

During an internship with IBM Research's Accelerated Materials Discovery group, where computer scientists and chemists build generative AI models together, I studied why interdisciplinary teams struggle to translate concepts across fields. Through eight interviews and four participatory design sessions, I found differences in tacit knowledge, terminology, and data structures that make it hard to capture chemical features for AI, and I explored scenario-based design and GPT as boundary objects, along with factsheets and datasheets for explainability, as ways to bridge them.

  • XAI in Interdisciplinary Research: Case of Accelerated Material Discovery
    Manuscript, with D. Zubarev

What's next

The next few years

Looking ahead, my work will stay focused on environmental and public health. I want to carry my citizen science work forward by integrating AI into community-led monitoring in ways that keep local knowledge in local hands, and to grow my collaborative autism research, carrying what I learned from the HIE work into it. I plan to publish in HCI, CSCW, digital government, and information systems venues, and to pursue funding through NSF and NIH programs on AI and community-engaged research.
