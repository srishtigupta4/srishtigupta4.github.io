Research

How communities and organizations build trust in AI under uncertainty

Across every project, the question underneath is the same one: does bringing people directly into the design of an AI system, not just consulting them, change whether it actually gets used, trusted, and made better over time?

Community science & sustainability

Dissertation research, Penn State · 2018–2023

Volunteer-run water-quality monitoring groups do real scientific work that fills gaps government agencies leave open, but most operate in isolation, reinventing solutions other groups already found. My dissertation designed community-science collaboratories, distributed infrastructure connecting these groups, and found that distributed collaboration is as much a governance and communication problem as a technical one. Partner organizations, including Lake Stewards of Maine and the Spring Creek Watershed Commission, adopted the resulting recommendations directly.

Indigenous AI

University of Nebraska Omaha · 2023–present

Indigenous communities face disproportionate exposure to natural disaster, compounded by a justified distrust of institutions, including AI systems trained on data that poorly represents Indigenous languages, culture, and values. In a think-aloud study with 18 Omaha Nation members, general-purpose chatbots consistently failed the cultural specificity Indigenous knowledge demands. In response, I co-designed TribalLLM, a retrieval-augmented chatbot built entirely on tribally-approved data, developed with tribal partners rather than for them. The throughline: trust in an AI system turned out to be inseparable from trust in the research relationship itself.

Public health & wellbeing

Health Management Informatics Lab · 2018–present

Families navigating Hypoxic-Ischemic Encephalopathy (HIE), a birth-related brain injury, manage fragmented specialist care and evolving prognoses at the most emotionally acute period of their lives. Using Uncertainty Management Theory, I found that caregivers and providers experience distinct, interacting forms of uncertainty, and that AI needs to help people manage that uncertainty together rather than pretend to resolve it. This lens runs through related work on autism screening and adult wellbeing, older adults' social circles and caregiving networks, and AI-powered telehealth.

What's next

Five-year outlook

Both the environmental-monitoring and public-health threads are moving from pilot studies into funded, multi-site collaborations, the former toward NSF's Smart & Connected Communities and Cyber-Human Systems programs, the latter toward NIH's NIMHD/NIMH programs on AI and community-engaged research for health disparities. The underlying bet stays the same: as AI embeds into nearly every job and daily experience, communities need a working understanding of how it behaves, and involving them directly in its design is the most reliable way to build that.
