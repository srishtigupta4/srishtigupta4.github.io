Human-Computer Interaction & UX Research

Hi, I'm Srishti.

I study how people, communities, and organizations under uncertainty and strain communicate, coordinate, and learn to trust the AI systems they increasingly depend on.

My work sits at the intersection of HCI, CSCW, and applied AI research. I spend most of my time with people who don't get asked what they need often enough: volunteer water-quality monitors, Indigenous emergency planners, and caregivers navigating a rare pediatric diagnosis. I use interviews, participatory design, and scenario-based methods to understand what they're actually up against, then design, build, and evaluate the AI systems meant to help.

I earned my Ph.D. in Information Sciences and Technology from Penn State, advised by Dr. John M. Carroll. I'm currently a Visiting Assistant Professor at the University at Albany, where I teach AI, data analytics, and AI ethics, and I'm looking for what's next: a research-faculty position, or a principal/lead UX research role where foundational, mixed-methods research shapes real product and strategy decisions.

A few things I'm proud of

A platform that outlived the study

Community-based environmental monitoring

My dissertation built the sociotechnical infrastructure connecting isolated, volunteer-run water-quality groups across North America, and found that distributed collaboration is as much a governance problem as a technical one. Partner organizations, including Lake Stewards of Maine and the Spring Creek Watershed Commission, adopted the resulting recommendations directly.

TribalLLM

Indigenous AI & data sovereignty

A retrieval-augmented chatbot built on tribally-approved data, co-designed with the Omaha Nation after fieldwork showed general-purpose chatbots couldn't meet the cultural specificity Indigenous knowledge demands. The bigger finding: trust in an AI system is inseparable from trust in the research relationship behind it.

HopeCare

Public health & caregiver communication

An LLM-based support tool for families navigating Hypoxic-Ischemic Encephalopathy, a birth-related brain injury. Using Uncertainty Management Theory, I found that caregivers and providers experience distinct, interacting forms of uncertainty, and that AI needs to help people manage that uncertainty together, not pretend to resolve it.
