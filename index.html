Human-Computer Interaction · Human-Centered AI · Community & Health Informatics

Hi, I'm Srishti.

I design and study intelligent systems with the communities who use them, so that technology solves real problems and leaves people with more agency, not less.

I am a Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) researcher with a passion for exploring the connections between technology and society. I earned my Ph.D. in Information Sciences and Technology from Penn State in 2023, where I was advised by Dr. John M. Carroll. I am currently a Visiting Assistant Professor at the Massry School of Business, University at Albany, SUNY, where I design and teach courses in AI, data analytics, and AI ethics for the AI for Business program.

My research sits at the intersection of HCI, Computer-Supported Cooperative Work (CSCW), and Intelligent User Interfaces (IUI). I focus on community and health informatics: how communities that are navigating uncertainty or strain, such as volunteer water-monitoring groups, Indigenous nations, and families facing medical crisis, collaborate, communicate, and increasingly rely on AI. I use qualitative and participatory methods, including interviews, observation, participatory design, scenario-based design, and focus groups, to design and evaluate those systems directly with the people who use them.

My work is driven by a commitment to creating technology that not only solves problems but also enriches lives and fosters a more connected and informed society. I believe community agency is not inevitable, even though AI increasingly is, so I study how to build systems that strengthen local knowledge rather than displace it.

I have 14 peer-reviewed journal and conference papers in venues including ACM CSCW, IUI, and DGO, and I have mentored nine students, from undergraduates to Ph.D. candidates.

A few things I'm proud of

Helping citizen scientists work together

Dissertation · Penn State, 2017–2023

Volunteer groups across the United States monitor the health of their local streams, yet roughly 70% of U.S. streams remain unassessed. I studied how these hyperlocal groups collaborate and what technology could help them share knowledge across a distributed infrastructure, through interviews, field observation, and participatory design work with groups in Pennsylvania and a national water data network. This work appears in ACM CSCW and Interacting with Computers.

Culturally grounded AI with tribal nations

Co-design with tribal communities · 2023–present

Together with tribal partners and collaborators, I have studied how large language models can support tribal emergency management and disaster resilience while remaining accountable to Indigenous data sovereignty and self-determination. The work, from fieldwork and co-design sessions through to the TribalLLM prototype, has been presented at ACM IUI and accepted at DGO 2026.

Building courses where none existed

University at Albany, SUNY · 2024–present

I designed all of my courses from scratch for the AI for Business program, from introductory Python programming to a graduate seminar on AI ethics. My students learn to question a result, interrogate an assumption, and reason about a system rather than simply operate it.
